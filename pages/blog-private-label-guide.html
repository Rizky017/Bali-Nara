How to Create Your Own Private Label Incense Brand

From choosing the right fragrance to finding a production partner, here's what to know before launching your own incense line.

Incense can be more than a product. For wellness brands, lifestyle businesses, retailers, and home fragrance companies, it can become part of a distinctive customer experience.

But how do you turn an idea into your own incense product?

Here are five things to consider before getting started.

1. Start With Your Brand Concept

Before choosing a product, think about who it is for and what experience you want to create.

Are you developing incense for everyday home use, a premium fragrance collection, or a product that complements your existing brand?

You don't need every detail figured out yet. A clear direction will help you communicate your needs and choose the right product.

2. Choose Your Fragrance and Product Type

Fragrance plays a central role in the identity of an incense product.

You might prefer familiar floral or woody notes, explore fresh and fruity profiles, or develop a scent direction that feels unique to your brand.

The incense format and production method matter, too. Materials, stick dimensions, fragrance formulation, and burning performance can all influence the finished product.

Whenever possible, request samples and test the actual incense before committing to a production order.

3. Understand MOQ and Packaging

Minimum order quantity (MOQ) determines how much you need to order at a time. It can vary depending on the product, fragrance, and customization requirements.

Before requesting a quotation, ask whether the MOQ applies per fragrance and whether different options can be combined in one order.

Packaging deserves the same attention. Basic packaging may be sufficient for an initial launch, while custom labels, boxes, or sleeves can help create a more distinctive brand experience.

Make sure you understand which packaging costs are included and which are charged separately.

4. Choose a Production Partner Carefully

The lowest price isn't always the best choice.

Look for a partner who communicates clearly, understands your product requirements, and can explain the production process. Ask how product specifications are documented, how quality is checked, and what happens if the finished product does not meet the agreed requirements.

Clear communication from the beginning can help prevent costly misunderstandings later.

5. Calculate the Full Cost

Your manufacturing price is only one part of the total cost.

Depending on your arrangement, you may also need to account for custom packaging, quality checks, freight, import duties, taxes, and fulfillment.

Request a quotation based on your intended quantity and specifications. This gives you a more realistic picture of your costs before setting your selling price.
